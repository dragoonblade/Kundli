📖 How Kundli Matching Works ▾

Ashtakoota Gun Milan is the traditional Vedic method for evaluating marriage compatibility. It compares the Moon nakshatras (birth stars) of two people across 8 dimensions called Kootas. Each Koota tests a different aspect of the relationship. The total score is out of 36 points. A score of 18 or above is generally considered acceptable. 24 or above is good. 32 or above is excellent. However, the total score is just one factor. Individual Koota scores, doshas, and the overall chart context all matter.

📋

How to evaluate holistically

The total score is not the whole picture

▾

Look at which Kootas scored well and which did not. A score of 20 with strong Nadi and Gana is better than 25 with Nadi Dosha. Pay attention to the top-weighted Kootas: Nadi (8), Bhakoot (7), and Gana (6) carry the most weight. Also consider the overall chart compatibility beyond just nakshatra matching.

🔍

Common myths debunked

What the numbers really mean

▾

Low score does not mean divorce. Many happy marriages have scores below 18. Compatibility is built through communication, respect, and effort, not just planetary positions.

Manglik after 28 is not automatic. The belief that Manglik Dosha disappears after age 28 is a simplification. The dosha's intensity may reduce, but it depends on the full chart context.

Both Manglik does not always cancel out. While two Manglik partners can neutralize the dosha, the specific Mars placements matter. Houses 1 and 7 are more significant than 2 and 12.

Nadi Dosha has exceptions. If the nakshatras are different even though the Nadi is the same, many astrologers consider the dosha reduced. Always look at the full picture.
